Department of Fish and Wildlife

Chapter 635

Division 42
COLUMBIA RIVER SYSTEM COMMERCIAL FISHERIES

635-042-0031
Early Fall Salmon Season

The early fall commercial salmon season in the Columbia River downstream of Bonneville Dam is assessed annually based on stock status and allocation policies. Harvest opportunity and gear regulations will be established by in-season modification.

(1) Salmon (except chum) and shad may be taken for commercial purposes in Zones 4 and 5, with the following exceptions:

(a) The deadline at the lower end of Zone 4 is defined as a straight line projected from the Warrior Rock Lighthouse on the Oregon shore easterly through the green navigation Buoy #1 and continuing to the Washington shore.

(b) Sanctuaries include the Washougal and Sandy river-mouth sanctuaries as described in 635-042-0005.

(c) The fishery is open as follows:

(A) 9:00 p.m. August 5 through 6:00 a.m. August 6;

(B) 9:00 p.m. August 9 through 6:00 a.m. August 10;

(C) 9:00 p.m. August 11 through 6:00 a.m. August 12;

(D) 9:00 p.m. August 13 through 6:00 a.m. August 14;

(E) 9:00 p.m. August 16 through 6:00 a.m. August 17;

(F) 9:00 p.m. August 18 through 6:00 a.m. August 19;

(G) 9:00 p.m. August 20 through 6:00 a.m. August 21;

(H) 9:00 p.m. August 24 through 6:00 a.m. August 25;

(I) 9:00 p.m. August 26 through 6:00 a.m. August 27; and

(J) 9:00 p.m. August 30 through 6:00 a.m. August 31.

(d) Gear is restricted as follows:

(A) Only drift gillnets with a 9-inch minimum mesh size are allowed; and

(B) Nets not authorized for this fishery may not be onboard.

(2) Adipose fin-clipped Chinook salmon, adipose fin-clipped coho salmon, sockeye salmon, pink salmon, and shad may be taken for commercial purposes in the mainstem Columbia River in Zones 1-3 with seine gear by those individuals possessing the proper licenses or permits issued by Oregon or Washington authorizing participation in the 2026 Emerging Commercial Fishery established through WAC 220-360-500. The required licenses and/or permits are an Emerging Fishery license and Experimental Fishery Permit issued by Washington or an Experimental Gear Permit issued by Oregon.

(a) The fishery is open:

(A) From 7:00 a.m. to 7:00 p.m. on weekdays from August 3 through October 16, except for closure on September 7 (Labor Day holiday).

(B) Notwithstanding the open hours shown in (2)(a)(A), active fishing may only occur for eight consecutive hours within each 12-hour period.

(b) Sanctuaries: Elokomin-B, Cowlitz, Kalama-B, and Lewis-B river-mouth sanctuaries as described in 635-042-0005 and Select Area commercial fishing sites.

(c) Gear:

(A) Beach or purse seine gear is allowed. Only one net is allowed per primary vessel.

(B) Mesh size restricted to a 3.5-inch maximum (inside of knot to outside of knot using hand tension stretched measure).

(C) Net material to consist of 3-strand nylon; twine size greater than or equal to #                    12.

(D) Seines may include a bunt of 1.0-2.0 inch knotless mesh.

(E) Net length not to exceed 200 fathoms (not including associated lead nets) and depth not to exceed 200 meshes (approximately 50 feet).

(F) No restrictions on corkline, leadline or use of stringers and slackers.

(G) A chafing strip panel consisting of non-monofilament webbing (such as nylon seine web or polyethylene trawl web) is allowed on bottom of net; maximum panel depth is five feet. Chafing mesh not to exceed 3.5-inch stretched measure for beach seines and 5-inch stretched measure for purse seines. There are no restrictions associated with hangings used to connect the net to the chafing panel or the net or chafing panel to the leadline or corkline.

(H) Red corks are required at 25-fathom intervals and red corks must be in contrast to the corks used in the remainder of the net.

(I) The use of one optional lead net is allowed per fishing operation. Lead nets may not exceed 100 fathoms in length or be vertically slackened. Seine and lead lines may not be connected. Lead nets must be retrieved daily. Lead nets may be constructed in either of the following configurations:

(i) 3.5-inch maximum mesh size webbing constructed with 3-strand nylon twine greater than or equal to #12; or

(ii) 14-inch minimum mesh size webbing constructed with nylon or cotton twine.

(d) Allowable sales are subject to Individual Fishing Quotas (as defined in the 2026 Experimental Fishery or Experimental Gear permits referenced in (2)). Retained Chinook and coho salmon must have a healed scar at the location of the clipped adipose fin. All legal adult Chinook and legal adult coho salmon caught must be kept and sold. Adult Chinook salmon are equal to or greater than 24-inches in total length. Adult coho salmon are equal to or greater than 20-inches in total length.

(e) Hand sorting or use of a knotless dip net is required for sorting. All fish must be sorted and/or released prior to removing entire seine from the water. Dry sorting is not permitted.

(f) Sort time is not to exceed 75 minutes:

(A) For beach seines, sort time is defined as the elapsed time from when the outer towed end of the net first contacts the shore or block until the net is emptied of fish.

(B) For purse seines, sort time is defined as the elapsed time from when all rings are pursed and out of the water until the net is emptied of fish.

(g) As a condition of fishing, an Oregon or Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife observer must be present at all times during fishing operations. Cooperation is required and includes transportation of observers to and from fishing site.

(3) Adipose fin-clipped Chinook salmon, adipose fin-clipped coho salmon, sockeye salmon, pink salmon, and shad may be taken for commercial purposes in the mainstem Columbia River in Zones 1-2 with a pound net by those individuals possessing the proper licenses or permits issued by Oregon or Washington authorizing participation in the 2026 Emerging Commercial Fishery established through WAC 220-360-500. The required licenses and/or permits are an Emerging Fishery license and Experimental Fishery Permit issued by Washington or an Experimental Gear Permit issued by Oregon.

(a) The fishery is open:

(A) From 7:00 a.m. to 7:00 p.m. on weekdays from August 3 through October 16, except for closure on September 7 (Labor Day holiday).

(B) Notwithstanding the open hours shown in (3)(a)(A), active fishing may only occur for eight consecutive hours within each 12-hour period.

(b) Legal gear is a pound net as described here:

(A) Lead and jigger netting may be no larger than 3 1/8-inch stretch mesh.

(B) The heart, spiller, tunnel, and live well (sorting chamber) netting may be no larger than 2 ½-inch stretch mesh.

(C) Pound net may have an optional apron of webbing no larger than 2 ½ inch stretch mesh extending out flat along the river bottom from the bottom of all netting components. The apron may not exceed 3 feet in depth on the lead and jigger, and the apron may not exceed 6 feet in depth on the heart. Aprons must be anchored or weighed down in a manner sufficient to hold the apron to the river bottom.

(D) Optional marine mammal gates may be used to prevent marine mammals from entering the heart during active fishing.

(E) When not actively fishing for less than 24 hours, the following configuration must be put in place to allow fish free passage and to prevent unintended corralling of fish in heart compartment:

(i) The opening of the heart along the lead must remain open. All spillers (pots) and live wells must be moved (i.e., lifted, removed, etc.,) in a manner that creates an opening providing egress from the heart.

(ii) A midpoint panel of the lead or the panel nearest to the shore must be lifted or removed to provide free passage around the lead. If the shore panel is chosen to be lifted/removed, the minimum water depth at the nearest shore piling with netting shall be no less than 10 feet at any given point in the tide.

(F) When not actively fishing for more than 24 hours but less than 72 hours, the following configuration must be put in place to allow fish free passage and to prevent unintended corralling of fish in heart compartment:

(i) The opening of the heart along the lead must remain open. All spillers (pots) and live wells must be moved (i.e., lifted, removed, etc.,) in a manner that creates an opening providing egress from the heart.

(ii) A midpoint panel of the lead and the panel nearest to the shore must be lifted or removed to provide free passage around the lead. The minimum water depth at the nearest shore piling with netting shall be no less than 10 feet at any given point in the tide.

(iii) Spot checks must be conducted daily by the permit holder during which   a logbook must be filled out. A WDFW or ODFW observer may be present during these daily spot checks.

(G) While not actively fishing for more than 72 hours but less than 120 hours, all netting must be raised above the high-water line.

(H) If the gear will not be fished for more than 120 hours, in addition to initially raising the netting above the high-water line, all netting must be removed from the piling within five days.

(c) Allowable sales are subject to Individual Fishing Quotas (as defined in the 2026 Experimental Fishery or Experimental Gear permits referenced in (3)). Retained Chinook and coho salmon must have a healed scar at the location of the clipped adipose fin. All legal adult Chinook and legal adult coho salmon caught must be kept and sold. Adult Chinook salmon are equal to or greater than 24-inches in total length. Adult coho salmon are equal to or greater than 20-inches in total length.

(d) Hand sorting or use of a knotless dip net is required for sorting. Dry sorting is not permitted.

(e) As a condition of fishing, an Oregon or Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife observer must be present at all times during fishing operations. Cooperation is required and includes transportation of observers to and from fishing site.

Statutory/Other Authority: ORS 496.118, ORS 506.109 & ORS 506.129
Statutes/Other Implemented: ORS 506.119 & ORS 507.030
