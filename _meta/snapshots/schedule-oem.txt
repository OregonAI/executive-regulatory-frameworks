Agency:  Oregon Department of Emergency Management (OEM)
Division:  Director’s Office
Program: Administration

Program Description
The Oregon Department of Emergency Management ( OEM) leads collaborative
statewide efforts, inclusive of all partners and the communities we serve, to ensure the
capability to get help in an emergency and to protect, mitigate, prepare for, respond
to, and recover from emergencies or disasters regardless of c ause. OEM coordinates
with partners from all levels of government, Tribal nations, non -governmental
organizations, and private sector businesses to synchronize efforts and ensure the state
leverages all available resources.
The OEM Director’s Office provides administration and direction over the emergency
management functions of OEM as prescribed by ORS 401.
Chief Resilience Office
The Chief Resilience Office leads OEM’s work to strengthen organizational and
statewide resilience, ensuring the agency can adapt and thrive in the face of disruption.
This portfolio integrates governance, strategic planning, preparedness, response,
recovery, and workforce culture, with a focus on making OEM itself a resilient institution
capable of leading during crisis.
A key pillar of this work is advancing Inclusion, Diversity, Equity, and Accessibility
(IDEA). Applying an IDEA lens internally not only improves equity but also reinforces
organizational resilience, helping OEM sustain operations, retain talent, and foste r
innovation. Externally, it guides collaboration with disability-led networks like Disability
Emergency Management Advisory Council (DEMAC), culturally specific organizations,
and other community -based partners to ensure emergency management systems
reflect and serve Oregon’s diverse communities.
Through initiatives ranging from targeted staff trainings  to accessible preparedness
campaigns, equitable granting processes, and innovation in multilingual alerts and risk
communication, the Chief Resilience Office combines organizational resilience with
community resilience. This integrated approach positions OEM as a national model for
how resilience is built from the inside out.
Internal Audit
OEM Internal Audit is responsible for evaluating the effectiveness of risk management,
control, and governance processes within the agency. It conducts independent and
objective audits and consulting engagements to ensure compliance with policies,
procedures, and regulations, and provides recommendations for enhancing
operational efficiency and effectiveness.

Strategic Communications and Partnerships
OEM Strategic Communications and Partnerships works to provide the right
information to the right people at the right time, in languages and formats that are
appropriate, accessible and relatable. Achieving this requires collaboration with other
sections to set communication priorities, while building partnerships with the private
sector partners and community-based organizations, as well as federal, state, local, and
Tribal jurisdictions.  The team ensures OEM speaks with one voice without
overwhelming the audience. Working with other sections is a high priority in setting
communication priorities and simplifying the process for getting information to the
community.
Strategic Communications and Partnerships is responsible for legislative tracking and
engagement, developing and maintaining strong relationships with the federally
recognized Tribal nations located within Oregon, building public-private partnerships,
managing public information requests, providing internal communication standards,
issuing official external communications across various OEM platforms and supporting
the development and maintenance of administrative rules.
Strategic Planning
Strategic planning is a comprehensive process used to define OEM’s direction and
allocate resources effectively. It establishes the department's vision and mission,
providing a clear sense of purpose and long -term goals, and includes an
environmental scan to analyze internal and external factors such as political, social, and
economic aspects. A SWOT analysis is used to identify the department's strengths,
weaknesses, opportunities, and threats. The analysis is used to set goals, which are
formulated into s trategies with detailed action plans to ensure effective
implementation, timeline outlines, responsibilities, and key milestones.
Progress is monitored and performance is evaluated against established metrics
through regular reviews and updates to the strategic plan, aligned with the state
budget biennium, to maintain its relevance and effectiveness and to promote
continuous improvement.

Agency:  Oregon Department of Emergency Management (OEM)
Division:  Business Services & Compliance
Program:  Finance

Program Description
OEM Finance provides Budget, Financial Accounting, Grants Management and
Procurement Services for OEM programs.
Budget
The Budget Analysts plan, manage and monitor OEM’s budget. This team develops
and executes the agency’s budget to ensure the agency is within the parameters
approved by the legislative body.  They work closely with all programs at OEM,
including other finance teams.
Financial Accounting
The Financial Accounting focuses its efforts on ensuring the agency is following the
Oregon Accounting Manual.  They monitor SPOTS, TEDS, Accounts Payable/
Receivable, and ensure payments to partners are made in a timely manner. This team
is responsible for all year -end activities and assists in creating the state’s Annual
Comprehensive Financial Report (ACFR).
Grant Management
The Grants Management team plays a specialized role, focusing specifically on the
financial aspects of managing grants. They ensure that grant funds are used as
intended and in compliance with all relevant regulations and reporting requirements
by tracking and monitoring grants, preparing all financial reporting, and reconciling
each grant during the duration of the grant period.
Procurement
The Procurement team  leads the strategic process of acquiring goods, services, and
raw materials from external suppliers. OEM’s Procurement Team is trained and
certified, as required by the Department of Administrative Services. Their goal is to
ensure compliance, identify any risks or needs of the program, and collaborate with
Department of Justice as needed.

Agency:        Oregon Department of Emergency Management (OEM)
Division:  Operations
Program:  Mitigation

Program Description
OEM Mitigation leads statewide efforts to reduce long -term risk from natural hazards.
Responsibilities include supporting communities in identifying, planning, and
implementing strategies that reduce disaster losses. Emphasis is placed on proactive,
long-term mitigation planning to enhance safety and resilience across Oregon. The
section administers and supports four primary program areas:
Hazard Mitigation Assistance (HMA)
Oversees FEMA’s HMA programs in Oregon, including the Hazard Mitigation Grant
Program (HMGP), Building Resilient Infrastructure and Communities (BRIC), and Flood
Mitigation Assistance (FMA). These programs fund cost -effective projects such as
seismic retrofits, flood mitigation, defensible space, and property acquisition to reduce
future disaster costs and impacts. FEMA may also introduce new programs that are part
of the HMA suite of grants.
Interagency Hazard Mitigation Team (IHMT)
Coordinates a multi-agency team of federal, state, Tribal, and local partners to support
mitigation activities before and after disasters. The IHMT helps integrate mitigation into
recovery efforts, identify statewide priorities, and promote cross -jurisdictional
collaboration.
Coordinates the development of Oregon Natural Hazards Mitigation Plan (NHMP)
updates, which provides statewide and regional information on the natural hazards
most likely to occur in the state with the goal of reducing the impacts of natural hazards
on people, property, and the environment.
Mitigation Planning
Provides technical assistance to state, local, Tribal, and territorial partners in
developing and maintaining FEMA -approved natural hazard mitigation plans. These
plans identify risks, assess vulnerabilities, and establish long -term strategies for
reducing future impacts. Planning efforts ensure eligibility for federal funding and help
integrate mitigation with broader community goals.
Risk Mapping, Assessment, and Planning (Risk MAP)
Facilitates access to updated hazard data, flood maps, and risk analysis tools in
coordination with FEMA. Risk MAP helps communities understand their exposure to

natural hazards and supports informed decision -making for land use planning,
emergency management, and mitigation investments.

Program:  Preparedness

Program Description
OEM Preparedness works to implement programs and opportunities to reach FEMA’s
national preparedness goal. The Integrated Preparedness Cycle of planning,
organizing/equipping, training, exercising, evaluating, and improving is a continuous
process that ens ures the regular examination of ever -changing threats, hazards, and
risks. The Cycle involves the assessment of threats, hazards, and risks, new and
updated plans, and improvements implemented by previously identified shortfalls or
gaps. The preparedness p riorities are developed to ensure that the needed
preparedness elements are incorporated. The program administers the State
Preparedness and Incident Response Equipment (SPIRE) grant program, which
provides equipment to local governments and other recipien ts for emergency
preparedness.

Agency:  Oregon Department of Emergency Management (OEM)
Division:  Operations
Program:  Public Assistance (PA)

Program Description
OEM Public Assistance (PA) coordinates the identification of disaster damage and
impacts within Oregon. If thresholds are met that exceed the capacity of the state’s
ability to handle the disaster, the PA team requests FEMA support to evaluate the
magnitude and severity of a disaster to determine whether supplemental federal
assistance is necessary. This data is included in a Governor’s request for federal
assistance.

The PA Program administers recovery and mitigation efforts oversight for the
supplementary federal share of the financial reimbursement assistance received
pursuant to 44 CFR 206.48 and the Stafford Act sections 406 and 404, to local and
state, special dis tricts, certain private non -profits, and federally recognized Tribes in
Oregon to remove debris, provide emergency protective measures, repair or replace
damaged public infrastructure and the creation of capped projects following a
disaster.

The PA Program also coordinates statewide recovery planning pursuant to ORS
401.052 and is responsible for the development and maintenance of the Oregon
Disaster Recovery Plan under the authority of ORS 401.

Agency:  Oregon Department of Emergency Management (OEM)
Division:  Operations
Program:  Response and Recovery

Program Description
OEM Response and Recovery directs the state’s operational activities during
emergencies and supports communities through recovery. The section is responsible
for developing and implementing Oregon’s Emergency Operations Plan (EOP),
leading pre-disaster planning, and maintaining strategies, action plans, and situational
analyses that ensure a coordinated and effective response and recovery when
incidents occur.

Recovery Operations
The Recovery Operations team leads long -term recovery in partnership with state,
federal, Tribal, and local governments, the private sector, and non -governmental
organizations. Together, they work to identify unmet needs, coordinate resources, and
implement long -term solutions to help communities rebuild stronger and more
resilient. The team supports Oregon’s Comprehensive Emergency Management Plan
by developing and maintaining the State Disaster Recovery Plan and its associated
Recovery Support Function annexes, supporting trainings and exercises, and providing
technical support and disaster recovery planning guidance to jurisdictions.
Regional Coordination
The Regional Coordination team works directly with county and local emergency
managers before, during, and after emergencies. They employ specialized field staff
members to provide technical support and guidance to Tribal and local emergency
management par tners through all areas and phases of emergencies and disasters.
Regional Emergency Management Coordinators provide service to all 36 counties and
nine federally recognized Tribes in Oregon across seven dedicated regions.
Response Coordination
The Response Coordination team operates the State Emergency Coordination Center
(ECC), coordinates statewide response efforts, supports local operations, and
manages search and rescue missions. The ECC provides an integrated and centralized
structure that utilizes a consistent and repeatable approach to coordinate and track the
emergency services activities of public and private organizations to stabilize and
restore impacted Community Lifelines. ECC activities are accomplished through
coordinated resource management, planning, situational assessment and information
sharing, operational coordination, and public information and warning.

Agency:  Oregon Department of Emergency Management (OEM)
Division:  Operations
Program:  Statewide Interoperability (SWI)

Program Description
OEM Statewide Interoperability supports the State Interoperability Executive Council
(SIEC). It works to implement various interoperable communications initiatives across
all government levels and throughout the state. The program focuses on public safety
broadband, land mobile radio, 9-1-1/NG-911, and alerts and warnings. Program work
includes developing and implementing statewide policy and guidance around alerts,
warnings, and notifications. Staff maintain the OR -Alert system and coordinate with
partners, including government personnel, first responders across the state, critical
infrastructure owner/operators, federal counterparts, and others. The program also
coordinates training for first responders related to emergency communications.
The program is responsible for issuing alerts, warnings, and notifications through the
OR-Alert system on behalf of county or state agencies when requested and as
necessary. These efforts play a crucial role in ensuring the effectiveness of public safety
communication systems in Oregon. They help create a cohesive statewide
communications infrastructure. SWI also provides emergency communications
support through the State’s Emergency Coordination Center as part of Emergency
Support Function 2 (Communications).
As part of its OR -Alert administrative duties, the  SWI also administers FEMA’s
Integrated Public Alert and Warning System (IPAWS) application process. Staff review
and approve COG applications for all local and state agencies. They also conduct
monthly tests of the system to ensure equipment and network functionality as required
by FEMA.
