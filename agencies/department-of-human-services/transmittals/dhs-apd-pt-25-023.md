Discussion/interpretation

The Oregon Legislature has directed Oregon Department of Human Services
(ODHS) to adopt a new model for calculating provider rates based on the
individualized needs of residents.
Effective January 1, 2026, Adult Foster Homes (AFH) and Residential Care Facilities
(RCFs) will have a rate methodology change. The new rate methodology does not
apply to AFHs or RCFs with Specific Needs Contracts or to Assisted Living Facilities
(ALFs). With this change, the new rate methodology will replace the current
methodology of base plus add-ons. Oregon ACCESS (OA) will automatically
calculate and apply the new methodology to assessments: no additional actions
are required.

An overview of these changes is provided in a training module, which may be
viewed here:

A webinar regarding this change is also under development and will be
announced in a separate transmittal once it is finalized.

The Office of Aging and People with Disabilities (APD) aims to support a person-
centered approach, increase transparency and reduce the need for hourly rate
exceptions. These changes are also designed to maintain quality of care for the
individuals we serve.

OA and the Mainframe systems will be updated to reflect these changes. A
separate transmittal will provide screenshots of the system changes and other
technical information.

Additional information regarding the methodology, rate changes (including
where to find the rate change on the 512), and exceptions process will be sent to
providers as a provider alert.

Point Calculation

Assessment points:

Each component of the CA/PS assessment will be given a number of points based
upon the assessed need level as follows:
  •   One (1) point for Independent
  •   Two (2) points for Minimal Assist
  •   Three (3) points for Assist
  •   Five (5) points for Substantial Assist
  •   Six (6) points for Full Assist

In most instances, if the assessment response has three choices (A, B, C), the
result is either Independent, Assist, or Full Assist. If the assessment response has
four choices (A, B, C, D), the result is either Independent, Minimal Assist,
Substantial Assist, or Full Assist.

Additional Cognition Points:

Individuals assessed as a Full Assist in Self-Preservation, Decision Making, and/or
Make Self Understood will receive an additional 10 points. Individuals assessed as
a Substantial or Full Assist in Challenging Behaviors will receive an additional 20
points. The maximum additional points cannot exceed 20 points.

Treatment Points:

Treatments that have triggered the Complex Medical Add-on will also have points,
based upon frequency. If the qualifying treatment is provided at least weekly, one
point will be provided. If the qualifying treatment is provided at least daily, three
points will be provided.

Additional Treatment information: Individuals who require sliding scale insulin
must have this treatment and the “Medications requiring skilled
assessment/judgment/monitoring” treatment indicated in the Treatment section
in CA/PS. In addition, adding treatments to an assessment that is in “Completed”
or “Administrative” status will not calculate the additional points.

Points Calculated into Tiers

The points described above will be added up to determine which tier the
individual is assessed at. A new assessment is not required to generate the new
tier. The systems will automatically convert to the new rate structure on 1/1/2026.
                   Assessed Levels       Score Ranges         Tier

                          Low                0-40            Tier 1

                      Moderate              41-55            Tier 2

                    Medium High             56-82            Tier 3

“Hold Harmless” Provision for Current Exceptions

Individuals who currently have an approved exception may have a total approved
rate (base plus add-on rate and the exception rate) that is higher or lower than
the tier rate for 2026.
If the total approved rate is lower than the tier rate, the exception will
automatically end, and the individual will be at the assessed tier rate. For
example, if an individual is currently at a total approved rate of $7,000 and is at
Tier five, the rate for this individual will be $7,773, starting January 2026.

If the total approved rate is higher than the tier rate, the individual will continue
with that total approved rate from 2025. For example, if an individual is currently
at a total approved rate of $12,000 and is at Tier five, the rate for this individual
will continue into January 2026 at the $12,000 rate. This rate will be maintained
until either an assessment shows a lower tier, the individual moves out of the
home, or during the renewal process, it is determined that the provider is not
providing the exceptional care through payroll records.

Providers with rates under this hold harmless provision will need to submit
payroll records, staffing plans, and form 514A to renew their exception under this
rate. The payroll records must reflect that the provider is staffing for:

  • The resident’s tier,
  • The resident’s exception, and
  • Other residents who have an exception (this includes staffing for the tier
     and exception hours)

If these records are not submitted by the time the exception expires, the hold
harmless provision will end. The provider will receive the consumer-assessed tier
going forward unless another exception is approved.

CBC New Exceptions Process:

AFH exception requests will only be granted when:

  • An individual’s needs exceed the caregiving hours as funded by a resident’s
     assessed tier, and
  • All other individuals receiving Medicaid LTSS are staffed according to the tier
     daily staffing requirements and any approved exceptions. This is in addition
     to a caregiver who is on duty 24 hours a day (i.e. the owner or the resident
     manager)

Example

A provider has four residents with the following tiers and exceptions:




If the provider wants to admit a fifth resident at Tier three and request an
exception, they must demonstrate—through payroll records—that they are
staffing at least one caregiver on duty 24 hours a day and providing a total of 36
caregiving hours per day.

An exception may be approved for 90-180 days and may require payroll records
from the provider if they have a history of inadequate staffing or if the exception
was approved before a resident moved into the AFH.

The department may reduce or terminate an exception when the individual’s
needs no longer justify the hours funded by the exception and/or when the
provider fails to submit the required documentation or does not provide the
additional staffing as authorized.
Process for requesting an exception or requesting a renewal of an exception:

The following must be submitted with each exception request by the AFH
provider:

  • The Department’s AFH Exceptions Calculator – The calculator will be
     provided on CM Tools, under the Long-Term Care Facilities section.
  • A current 514 – An updated 514 will be provided on CM tools, under the
     Long-Term Care Facilities section. The version on the forms server will be
     updated later.
  • The following payroll documentation:
        o Pay stubs showing hours worked and taxes withheld
        o Documentation of rate of pay
        o Receipts of payroll and employer tax submission to the Internal
            Revenue Service (IRS) and the Oregon Department of Revenue (DOR)
        o Time sheets verifying when caregivers work designated shifts. These
            timesheets must be signed by the caregiver.
        o The department may request additional documentation if the
            department has concerns about the validity of the documents
            submitted.

Note: Local office staff should ensure that providers are including each of the
payroll records indicated above, however staff are not expected to verify the
records in detail.

Local/branch action required
Local office staff must ensure the following steps are completed before
submitting an exception request:

  • All documentation and information required by this transmittal has been
     submitted by the provider.
  • All provider-submitted documentation is date-stamped if received via fax or
     in-person.

  • A new CA/PS assessment must be conducted if the exception request
     indicates increased needs that conflict with the current assessment. This
     requirement may be temporarily waived in urgent situations or when the
     individual is discharging from a hospital. In such cases, the exception may
     be approved for up to 90 days, and a CA/PS assessment must be completed
     before the exception can be renewed.

  • A supervisor or lead worker has reviewed the documentation and CA/PS
     assessment and agrees the exception is justified and the CA/PS assessment
     has been updated as needed.

Note: Local offices may deny exception requests. In such cases:

  • The provider must receive a written explanation for the denial.

  • If denying a renewal of an existing exception:

        o The provider must receive a 10-day written notice before the exception
           ends, including justification for the denial.

        o Central Office must be notified to ensure the rate is stopped at the
           appropriate time. This may require extending the rate to allow for the
           full 10-day notice.

        o If the denial is due to insufficient staffing, the provider must have been
           given 14 days to submit an updated staffing plan before the denial.

Additional responsibilities:

  • Send all required documents to Central Office for a determination.

  • If Central Office requests additional documentation (e.g., payroll records),
     local office staff must communicate these requirements to the provider.
   • Once Central Office approves or denies the exception, local office staff must
      notify the provider of the decision.

Exceptions submitted prior to January 1st, 2026, will still be processed and will be
approved with two different rates. If approved, one rate will reflect the exception
hours and rate from the effective date to December 31st, 2025. The second rate
will show the rate and any exceptional hours approved after January 1st, 2026.




Central office action required

Regarding AFH Exceptions, Central Office staff will:

   • Review exception requests submitted by local offices.

   • Request additional documentation from local offices as needed.

   • Review payroll documentation.

   • Provide written determinations to local staff for communication to
      providers.
