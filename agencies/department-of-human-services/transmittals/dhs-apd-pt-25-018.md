Discussion/interpretation
This policy transmittal explains how limited animal care, as well as other incidental activities
(defined below), may be provided by Homecare Workers (HCWs) or In-Home Care Agencies
(IHCA) for individuals receiving in-home services who need this level of support from their
provider(s).
Incidental activities are brief, simple chores that an HCW or IHCA may complete during their
shift that may not be stated on the task list. They must:
   • Be tied to the individual’s documented ADL/IADL need.
   • Support the individual’s health, safety, or home environment.
   • Benefit the individual only (not others in the household).
   • Not add extra or exceptional service hours.


In addition:
    • ADL/IADL tasks always take priority over incidental activities.
    • Providers are not required to perform incidental activities they are uncomfortable with
      (e.g., due to physical limitations, safety concerns, or preference).
    • Exceptions are not granted beyond the listed parameters.




Implementation/transition instructions
Limited Animal Care:

Assistance with an individual’s animals in their home is permitted only when directly related to
an assessed ADL or IADL need and documented in the task list.

   1) Allowable Animal Care
      Providers may help only when care relates to the individual’s ADL/IADL needs, including

         •   Assisting the individual while walking their animal when the individual cannot
             safely walk the animal on their own.
         •   Cleaning, disinfecting, and disposing of indoor waste.
         •   Putting food or water in a dish.
         •   Shopping for animal supplies.
         •   Letting the animal in/out of the home.
         •   Giving oral medicine in food or water.
         •   Accompanying the individual to veterinary appointments when ADL/IADL support
             is needed.
                 o The individual must plan provider hours to cover travel time and time at te
                    veterinary appointment.
                 o Mileage reimbursement requires case manager authorization.


   2) Non-Allowable Animal Care
      Providers may not:

         •   Care for animals that do not live in the individual’s home.
          •   Pick up feces outside the home unless the animal waste poses a health hazardous
              to the individual or others entering/exiting the home.
          •   Care for animals outside the home (e.g., chickens, farm animals).

    3) Health & Safety Note
       If animals regularly soil the home and create unsafe conditions, providers must notify the
       case manager and submit the Online Health and Safety Reporting Form (APD-AR-24-010).



Change on Task List form:

The task list (SDS 598N) will be modified in a future Oregon ACCESS release to include updated
and additional information at the end of the document (the release date is currently being
determined). The update is provided below.



NOTE: The task list reflects the authorized tasks, hours, and mileage for the provider. The
hours and mileage may be prorated if the provider is not authorized to work for an entire pay
period.


A pay period consists of two consecutive work weeks. A work week is defined as Sunday 12:00
a.m. through Saturday 11:59 p.m. This does not apply to in-home care agencies.


Tasks and hours authorized on this task list are for the benefit of the individual only, and do
not include care for others in the household. This includes but is not limited to; childcare or
chores outside the home (i.e., yard work).


Providers may offer minimal care for an individual’s indoor animal(s) but are not required to
do so. This does not include exercise without the individual present, outdoor cleanup unless
necessary for the safety and health of the individual, care for farm animals, or extensive time
with multiple animals.

Local/branch action required
Local office (LO) supervisors should share this information with staff and individuals receiving
in-home services. The APD Training Unit (TUFSS) will incorporate incidental activities into
training modules for case managers and other staff as needed. The Oregon Home Care
Commission (OHCC) and Carewell (SEIU) will also include this information in their training for
homecare workers.

Central office action required
This information will be posted on the In-Home Services page of the APD Case Management
Tools and Resources website and shared with IHCA leadership. TUFSS will be supported in
developing any necessary training materials on this topic.
