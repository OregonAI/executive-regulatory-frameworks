Discussion/interpretation
There has been increased activity by U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) in
Oregon. This may affect individuals we serve and those who support them. The purpose of this
policy transmittal is to provide guidance to Aging and People with Disabilities (APD) and Area
Agency on Aging (AAA) staff on how to respond when ICE engages with:


   •   An individual receiving Medicaid Long Term Services and Supports (LTSS) through APD;
   •    An Adult Foster Home (AFH) owner/operator; or
   •    A Home Care Worker (HCW).


This transmittal supports continuity of care, timely communication, and consistent
documentation. It does not provide legal advice. Links to additional resources are provided at
the end of this transmittal.



Scenario Based Guidance for Staff

When staff learns that ICE has detained or is investigating a person connected to APD services,
support the individual and respond within your role. Staff cannot interfere with or guarantee
the outcome of any ICE action or immigration proceeding.

Scenario One – ICE detains an individual receiving Medicaid LTSS

Staff should:

       1. Confirm basic facts and contacts
             o Confirm who was detained (individual receiving services), when and how APD
                 learned of the detainment, and the individual’s current location, if known.
             o If the individual has a representative, attempt to contact them regarding the
                 detainment.
       2. Staff and notify
             o Staff the situation with a local office supervisor and others as appropriate to
                 determine needed coordination and any immediate health and safety concerns.
             o Notify local eligibility staff about the detainment so they can plan for appropriate
                 actions in ONE if the individual does not return.
             o Complete a ROSE form.
             o Provide notification to the APD Service Delivery Administrator (Christy Shipman)
                 and other Senior APD Leadership.
             o Review the service plan, identifying and notifying the appropriate providers that
                 services must be ended.
       3. Review and coordinate benefits and payments
             o Take system actions to end LTSS provider payments as appropriate.
             o Do not take actions to close LTSS services or medical benefits unless it is
                 confirmed the individual will not return.
             o If extended waiver eligibility (EWE) services were open on the date of detainment
                 and the individuals services will be closed, email
                 apd.ewe.request@odhsoha.oregon.gov (include name, prime, and date of
                 detainment).
    4. Decision notices
          o Issue required decision notices (540 and 540ICP, if applicable) using the
             rule/reason language outlined in the “Decision notices when services close due
             to ICE detainment” section below.
    5. Support communication and continuity of care
          o Encourage the facility/provider and any representative to share necessary
             information with legal counsel, providers, or staff providing health care services,
             as appropriate.
    6. Document
          o Narrate all actions in Oregon ACCESS (OA), including how APD learned of the
             detainment, steps taken, and the follow-up plan.
          o Schedule a follow-up staffing or case review, if needed, to reassess benefit status
             and service needs.
    7. Avoid legal advice
          o If the individual or representative asks what to do about immigration
             proceedings, explain that APD cannot provide legal advice and refer them to
             legal resources.

Scenario 2 – ICE detains an Adult Foster Home (AFH) owner/operator

(Note: For other facility-based settings, the facility administrator or designee must have a plan
to ensure care is minimally disrupted if a staff member is detained; staff should support
individuals within established roles and processes.)

Staff should:

   1. Notify and staff
         o Notify Safety, Oversight and Quality (SOQ) at
             safetyoversight.andquality@odhsoha.oregon.gov and the Local Licensing
             Authority (LLA) of the provider’s detainment.
         o Complete a ROSE form.
         o Staff the situation with a local office supervisor, SOQ/LLA, and others as
             appropriate to determine needed coordination, including:
                ▪ Whether residents (including the individual(s) on your caseload) need to
                   move or other back-up plan alternative;
                ▪ Availability of natural supports; and
                ▪ Immediate care needs (for example, medications, specialty providers,
                   payment sources).
         o Provide notification to the APD Service Delivery Administrator (Christy Shipman)
             and other Senior APD Leadership.
   2. Plan for placement and services
         o If it does not appear the provider will return shortly after detainment:
                ▪  Begin looking for alternative placement(s) for residents.
               ▪ Determine whether any new placement is temporary or long-term and set
                   up appropriate payment authorizations.
               ▪ Coordinate with families/natural supports as available.
   3. End and re-establish services
         o Adjust the service plan authorization as appropriate for individuals receiving
            Medicaid services who move into a new placement.
   4. Document and coordinate
         o Narrate all actions in OA, including SOQ/LLA guidance, placement decisions, and
            coordination with residents and families.
   5. Avoid legal advice
         o If residents or families ask about the AFH owner/operator’s immigration case,
            explain that APD cannot provide legal advice and refer them to legal resources.

Scenario 3 – ICE detains a Home Care Worker (HCW)

Staff should:

   1. Confirm and staff
         o Obtain available details about the detainment from any available source.
         o Staff with the local office supervisor and others as appropriate to determine
             needed coordination to ensure individual service needs are met.
         o Provide notification to the APD Service Delivery Administrator (Christy Shipman)
             and other Senior APD Leadership.
   2. Identify and support impacted individuals
         o Determine all individuals for whom the HCW has been providing LTSS.
         o Notify and support each impacted individual; encourage them to implement their
             back-up plan where one exists and discuss short-term options for maintaining
             services.
         o Adjust service plans as appropriate and assist with finding and authorizing new
             providers.
   3. End HCW-related payments and contacts
         o End ONGO and unlink the individual from the HCW in Provider Time Capture (PTC)
             and other applicable payment systems, following standard procedures and
             effective dates.
         o If it is learned the HCW will not return after detainment, the CM or local HCW
             coordinator must submit form DHS 2680 (on the form server) to
             hcw.terminations@odhsoha.oregon.gov to evaluate termination of the HCW’s
             provider number.
         o If the HCW was assisting an Independent Choices Program (ICP) participant,
             complete and submit a DHS 548 to icp.spd@odhsoha.oregon.gov (include the
            HCW’s last day working). Let the individual know they should begin searching for a
            new provider.
   4. Document
         o Narrate all actions in OA and confirm any required ROSE or internal reporting has
            been completed.
   5. Avoid legal advice
         o When individuals ask what this means for the HCW’s immigration status, explain
            that APD cannot advise on immigration matters and refer them to legal resources.

Decision notices when services close due to ICE detainment

When ICE detainment results in an individual not returning to their service setting, CMs must
ensure an appropriate decision notice is issued to close services (notification to eligibility staff is
also required for any medical/SNAP benefits). This section summarizes the required notice
language and forms for a service closure:

A 540 Notice of Planned Action must be issued as follows:

      •   To receive Long-Term Services and Supports from the State of Oregon, you must be
          living in Oregon. According to the information we received, you no longer reside in
          Oregon. Your services are closed effective mm/dd/yy. We made these decisions and
          issued this notice per authority of Oregon Administrative Rules (OARs) 461-120-0010.

In addition, if the individual was receiving services through the Independent Choices Program,
issue a 540ICP Notice to end the benefits at the end of the month.

      •   In the 540ICP, select “See rules and reasons below” and enter the same rule/reason
          language above.
      •   Send a copy of the 540ICP to icp.spd@odhsoha.oregon.gov to inform the ICP Policy
          Analyst.

Ensure the effective dates on the 540/540ICP align with OA, MMIS, 512, ONE, CEP/PTC, and any
other system changes. Narrate that the notice was issued, including the reason for closure and
effective date.

Note: Use language found in the Decision Notice – Service Decision Notice Preparations Tips
guide as needed. As previously mentioned, staff may not take adverse action to close
medical/services until we know for sure the individual is not going to return to their service
setting.

Local/branch action required
Begin communicating and taking actions as described above immediately when any
situations like those described above occur.

Central office action required
Communicate with providers for LTSS individuals, individuals (LTSS eligible individuals), local
office staff, supervisors and Central Office staff regarding the individual’s detainment.
