Discussion/interpretation
This policy transmittal provides new guidance for APS workers when investigating
reported abuse in Assisted Living Facilities (ALF) and Residential Care Facilities
(RCF).
When conducting Facility APS investigations, OAR 411-020-0120(5)(d) requires APS
staff to confirm that immediate protection for facility residents is in place and
obtain and document a safety plan from the provider to correct any problem
immediately. APS staff must also communicate with SOQ as needed on issues or
concerns about the facility’s safety plan.


This policy provides a new Facility Safety Plan (FSP) form for APS to give to the
provider to document what steps they have taken, or will be taking, to ensure
resident safety after a reported incident of abuse.


Current Process


APS staff are currently trained to ask the provider, upon APS arrival at the facility,
what steps the provider has taken in response to the reported incident to ensure
resident safety, both for the alleged victim and for other residents as needed (e.g.
systemic reviews after a series of medication errors.) APS eventually documents
those steps reported by the provider in the “Service Provider Actions” section in
the Centralized Abuse Management (CAM) system.


Currently, APS communicates any immediate concerns about the adequacy of the
service provider’s actions to the APD Safety, Oversight, and Quality (SOQ) unit by
various means. In some instances, a Report of Serious Event (ROSE) form may be
submitted to alert SOQ to a serious incident and any ongoing safety concerns.
Less imminent concerns may be communicated to SOQ staff via email, telephone,
or referral to the SOQ Licensing Complaint Unit (LCU).


The lack of a consistent format for documenting the service provider’s actions
that can be quickly and easily shared can result in uncertainty and delays in
determining what, if any, immediate intervention or regulatory action by SOQ
should be taken in response to the incident.
New Process


This policy transmittal provides a new, consistent, format for APS to gather and
more easily share the steps the provider has taken, or intends to take, to ensure
safety after a reported incident of abuse as required by rule.



Implementation/transition instructions
When providing APS intervention in an ALF or RCF as part of an assigned
investigation, the APS worker must:


  1. Advise facility staff or administration of the allegation and any potential
     associated risks to the alleged victim or other residents.

  2. Ask facility staff to complete and sign a Facility Safety Plan (FSP) form on the
     alleged incident, documenting what steps they have taken, or intend to take,
     to ensure safety considering the reported incident. Advise them that the FSP
     must be completed during the initial APS visit.

  3. Meet with the alleged victim to assess their immediate safety and need for
     protective services, including the APS worker’s own determination of the
     impact of the reported incident on the alleged victim, their understanding of
     their situation, and their wishes for intervention.

  4. Meet with facility staff again to review the FSP, ask any questions needed to
     clarify the actions the provider has taken (or will be taking) and request any
     needed written clarifications on the form. Email any concerns about the
     adequacy of the FSP to SOQ at CBC.CorrectiveAction@odhsoha.oregon.gov,
     and staff any immediate safety concerns with the local APS supervisor.


  5. Proceed with the investigative process – interviews, collecting records, etc.
  6. Upload the FSP to the case in CAM within 24 hours of the initial visit. Write a
     summary of it in the Service Provider Actions section of CAM to capture the
     facility’s actions in the final investigation report.

  7. If a ROSE form has not already been submitted, assess whether the reported
     incident and initial information gathered during the initial visit meet the
     criteria for a completion of a ROSE form. If so, the investigator must
     complete a ROSE form and submit it to the APD Safety Team as required
     with the FSP attached.

  8. During the investigation, discuss any concerns about the ongoing
     effectiveness of the FSP with the provider. Ask them to complete an updated
     form as needed to capture any new steps they have taken or intend to take.

  9. If new incidents or allegations are discovered or reported during
     investigation, repeat steps 2-8 above to gather and document a new FSP
     related to the new allegation, unless the new allegation involves the exact
     same incident that is already addressed in the original FSP.

 10. Communicate with SOQ via email at CBC.CorrectiveAction@odhsoha.oregon.gov
     during the investigation to report any significant changes to the condition of
     the alleged victim (e.g. AV has died as a result of the incident) or other
     information that SOQ may need to assess the need for regulatory action.


Training/communication plan
The policy transmittal will be posted at the APS Staff Tools website, APD’s
Transmittals website, and the CAM Knowledge tab with other APS policy
transmittals. Providers will be alerted to the new requirement by an SOQ
Provider Alert. Communication and discussion regarding this policy transmittal
will occur during APS supervisor meetings, scheduled APS Training events, and
other venues as needed. Requests for technical assistance on applying this policy
transmittal should be sent to the Central APS Unit at
APS.TechAssistance@odhsoha.oregon.gov.



Local/branch action required
Please discuss these changes with staff during huddles and staff meetings.
Ensure local APS workers awareness of the policy transmittal and where it is
posted on the APD Transmittals Public Facing website.

Central office action required
Respond to inquiries regarding this policy transmittal as needed.
