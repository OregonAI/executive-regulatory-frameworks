Organizational Placement       Schedule #: 2002-0020
Agency:  Oregon Department of Transportation (ODOT)
Division:  Transportation Safety (TSD)

Program Description:
The Transportation Safety Division (TSD) provides information, direct services, technical expertise, and
grants and contracts to the public and to partner agencies and organizations working to increase
transportation safety. More than half the funding comes from federal highway funds designated for
safety programs. The Division administers more than 550 grants and contracts each year to agencies,
programs, and groups, which deliver transportation safety related programs to Oregon citizens. The
Division operates under ORS’s 184.615, 802.110 - .345, and OAR 737.

Operations provides support, technical and informational services, staffing, and oversight for the field
programs and TSD administration. Personnel work with partner agencies, advisory committees, local
traffic safety committees, and numerous volunteer, community, and non-profit entities, which are
dedicated to increasing the safety of Oregonians upon highways. Field Programs encompass service
programs, which are responsible for providing technical expertise, guidance, grants, and contract
delivery, assistance in securing program funding, development, and implementation of the transportation
safety program areas fostered and managed by the Transportation Safety Division.

The Division works to improve safety upon state highways through promoting and supporting education,
enforcement, and engineering efforts addressing transportation safety issues.

Program Description:
The Bicyclist Safety program is responsible for increasing bicyclist safety by promoting public
education and awareness through presentations, information campaigns, training, and supporting
enforcement efforts on bicycle safety issues involving stakeholders, law enforcement personnel, and the
public at large. The program provides technical assistance and advice concerning safety issues, federal
and state regulations, programs, and resources available to government agencies and the public. In
addition, the Bicyclist Safety program provides grant funding to local grass roots educational efforts by
promoting safe bicycling. The Bicyclist Safety program operates under ORS 802.325.

The Bicyclist Safety program has identified a number of issues and problems relating to bicyclist safety
in Oregon and has the goal of reducing injuries suffered by bicyclists in crashes by developing strategies
intended to increase public awareness and education in addition to promoting informed, effective
enforcement by public safety agencies. The Bicycle and Pedestrian Advisory Committee, part of the
Bicycle and Pedestrian program in ODOT’s Highway Division Traffic Management Section, provides
oversight and advice on program goals and objectives.

Program: Employer Safety

Program Description:
The Employer Safety program is responsible for assisting and encouraging employers to promote
transportation safety within their workforce by providing public information and training to employers
and safety officers who in turn provide training and information to employees concerning work related
transportation safety issues. The program intends for employees to share the information received at
work on transportation safety issues with family members in an effort to reduce the number of employee
families involved in crashes and the associated costs to businesses’ in human and financial resources.

Nationally, traffic crashes are the leading on the job injury and the Employer Safety program seeks to
reduce the number of on the job vehicle related crashes while increasing the number of companies
participating in proactive safety efforts. The Employer Safety program provides funding and resources
to ensure training and awareness of employees and their family’s on safety issues concerning motor
vehicles.

The program is funded under Section 402 of the Transportation Equity Act for the 21st Century, which
provides funds to assist states, counties, and communities in proactive efforts to mitigate traffic safety
issues.

Program: Impaired Driving (Driving Under the Influence of Intoxicants [DUII])

Program Description:
The Impaired Driving program seeks to reduce the percentage of traffic fatalities involving alcohol or
other drugs. The program is responsible for assisting to maintain and enhance DUII enforcement levels,
ensure the availability and use of up-to-date DUII enforcement technologies and practices, and increase
the number of Oregon certified Drug Recognition Experts.

The program provides assistance and support to establishing and maintaining an effective, aggressive
system of programs and efforts aimed at the reduction of traffic crash fatalities due to impaired vehicle
operation. The program supports the creation and implementation of DUII related statutes, DUII
enforcement, and the prosecution and adjudication of DUII offenders by providing funding and technical
expertise to existing programs which act to counter the threat on Oregon roadways posed by DUII
drivers. In addition, the program manager is a member of the Oregon DUII Multi-Disciplinary Task
Force, which is comprised of government and civilian representatives fostering education and discussion
on DUII matters. The program manager staffs the Governors Advisory Committee on DUII.

Program Description:
The Motorcycle Safety program is responsible for promoting safe motorcycling in Oregon with a goal to
steadily reduce the number of traffic crashes involving motorcycles. The program provides information
to the public through public service announcements, publications, telecommunications, and personnel
contact informing the public on issues relating to drinking and riding, licensing, protective gear, speed,

training and education of motorcyclists. The program provides oversight and funding to TEAM
OREGON, established in 1984 as a cooperative effort between the Oregon Department of
Transportation and Oregon State University.  TEAM OREGON provides accessible training for
beginning and experienced motorcyclists at locations throughout Oregon as part of the Motorcycle
Safety program’s mission. TEAM OREGON is staffed and managed by Oregon State University.

Program: Occupant Protection

Program Description:
The Occupant Protection program is responsible for promoting the correct use of restraint devices
including safety belts and child safety seats to reduce the number of crash related injuries, their severity,
and reduce fatalities. The program participates in the 3 FLAGS program which combines efforts of
Oregon, Washington, and British Columbia law enforcement agencies to conduct enforcement blitzes
upon highways and interstates, delivers public information and education on the needs and benefits of
restraint devices, provides overtime and training funding for law enforcement personnel to increase the
effectiveness and presence of enforcement personnel, and provides funding for the Oregon Child Safety
Seat Resource Center. The center provides hands on training in the use and application of child restraint
devices. The center trains and certifies car seat inspection volunteers who provide free safety checks of
child car seats.

The Occupant Protection program is in response to ORS 811.210 - .220 requiring the use of restraint
devices for vehicle occupants and accepted procedure for issuance of exemptions to using restraint
devices

 Organizational Placement       Schedule #: 2002-0020
Agency:  Oregon Department of Transportation (ODOT)
Division:  Transportation Safety
Program: Region Transportation Safety Coordinators

Program Description:
The Region Transportation Safety Coordinators, staffing ODOT’s five regional offices, are responsible
for providing advocacy concerning local transportation safety issues, assisting with identifying traffic
safety problems and high risk areas, and helping to determine appropriate action for the reduction and
elimination of traffic safety issues. In addition, staff members are responsible for coordinating local
safety activities and acting as liaison to Division transportation safety programs while providing
information and education to local communities on transportation safety issues and fostering local
community interaction to address and solve safety issues and problems.

The Transportation Safety Coordinators work closely with Traffic Section personnel in their assigned
regions to heighten awareness among Traffic personnel to the regions transportation safety issues and to
foster the inclusion of safety related programs and issues into regional design, engineering, and
maintenance work. The goal of Traffic Safety Coordinators is to reduce the number of traffic fatalities
and injuries attributed to any cause. Traffic Safety Coordinators work to identify high-risk areas of state
highways and provide oversight and direction for the creation of Traffic Safety Corridors.

Currently Region 1 contains eleven cities, three counties, and two unincorporated areas having
established Local Traffic Safety Committees or similar action groups. Drivers log in excess of
18,000,000 miles per day in travel on Region 1’s Highways. Region 1 is predominantly urban in nature.
Currently Region 1 has three safety corridors and two truck safety corridors.

Region 2 is comprised of nine counties with five established safety corridors and two established truck
safety corridors. Currently seventeen cities and one county have Local Traffic Safety Committees.
Region 2 is a mix of urban and rural areas.

Region 3 contains five counties distinctly rural in nature. The rural nature of the region and low socio-
economics reflect in the traffic safety issues and problems. Region 3 has a higher percentage on average
of traffic fatalities than anywhere else in Oregon with alcohol present in a significant portion of fatal
crashes. Currently ten Local Traffic Safety Committees are active.

Region 4 encompasses nine counties, is rural in nature, and contains 1,982 miles of state highway.

Region 5 encompasses eight counties with 2,108.27 miles of state highway. Currently there are four
Traffic Safety Committees, or similar bodies, active in the region and two active Safety Corridors. The
region is rural in nature.
