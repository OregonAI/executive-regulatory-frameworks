Program Description
The Civil Rights Program within the Oregon Health Authority’s (OHA) Equity and
Inclusion Division acts as the Director’s designee for OHA to investigate all employee
complaints of discrimination, harassment, intimidation or retaliation under the DAS
Discrimination and Harassment Free Workplace Policy. This is a separate process
from Human Resources which makes OHA distinct from other state agencies.

In addition, the Civil Rights Program also serves as OHA’s designee for public
complaints of discrimination, harassment, or retaliation under OAR 943-005-0010.

Program Description
The Traditional Health Workers (THW) Program is responsible to engage and provide
technical assistance to health system partners, including community-based
organizations to advance quality and responsive care using traditional health workers
(THWs). As of current, Oregon recognizes the following categories as part of the THW
workforce: birth doula, community health worker, peer support specialist, peer
wellness specialist, and personal health navigators. Additionally, the THW
Commission and three (3) subcommittees (Systems Integration, Payment Model, and
Training Evaluation Metrics and Program Scoring) advise and make
recommendations on THW-related activities.

Section: Race, Ethnicity, Language, and Disability (REALD) & Sexual Orientation
and Gender Identity (SOGI)

Program Description
In 2013 ORS 413.161 passed which required Oregon Health Authority (OHA) and
Oregon Department of Human Services (ODHS) to advance health equity by
developing and standardizing race, ethnicity, language, and disability (REALD) data
collection across OHA and ODHS. In 2021, HB 3159 passed, which amended ORS
413.161 to include sexual orientation and gender identity (SOGI) data collection
standards as well.

Additionally, HB 3159 resulted in three new ORS’:

• ORS 413.163: Build a data registry for both REALD & SOGI that allows
individuals licensed, certified, registered or otherwise authorized to practice by
a board, coordinated care organizations (CCOs), and insurers to electronically
submit data collected under ORS 413.164; and query the data registry to
determine whether the registry contains current data for a patient, member or
client. This registry must also allow a patient, member or client to directly
submit to the data system their data described in this section.
• ORS 413.164: Develop and implement reporting requirements for providers,
coordinated care organizations and health insurers reporting
 REALD & SOGI
data.
• ORS 413.166: Create a grant program to help community partners and
community-based organizations serving underrepresented populations
reporting this data.
The REALD & SOGI Section in the Equity and Inclusion Division of OHA oversees the
development of the Registry scheduled to launch in 2027. The Registry is the ‘front
door’ for inputs. The existing REALD & SOGI Repository managed by REALD & SOGI
Section in collaboration with dedicated staff from the Office of Information Services
(OIS), stores and processes REALD & SOGI collected from internal quality data
sources in OHA as well as providers and individuals using other pre-existing input
mechanisms such as the Public Facing Survey Tool (PFST).

OHA gathers and analyzes the data, disseminates information, and produces reports
using REALD & SOGI data. OHA also releases the data through a request process led
by the REALD & SOGI Program in consultation with the REALD & SOGI Data
Governance Committee. Entities within OHA and ODHS and external entities such as
community-based organizations, research groups, and Coordinated Care

Organizations may request REALD & SOGI data from the Repository. Requests are
evaluated based on their intent to advance health and service equity, per OHA and
ODHS mission statements. All requests for identifiable data must qualify under at
least one HIPAA activity designation.
