Agency: Criminal Justice Commission
Program: Criminal Justice Commission

Program Description
The Oregon Criminal Justice Commission (CJC) is responsible for improving the efficiency and
effectiveness of state and local criminal justice systems by providing a centralized and impartial
forum for statewide policy development and planning. The nine member commission is charged
with developing and maintaining a state criminal justice policy and comprehensive, long-range
plan for a coordinated state criminal justice system that encompasses public safety, offender
accountability, crime reduction and prevention and offender treatment and rehabilitation; serving
as a clearinghouse for the collection, preparation, analysis and dissemination of information on
the state’s criminal justice system; providing technical assistance to local public safety
coordinating councils (LPSCCs); and administering grants for specialty courts and Justice
Reinvestment.

The commission is also responsible for maintaining the state’s criminal sentencing guidelines
established to create guidelines for Oregon sentencing proceedings. The sentencing guidelines
are intended to remedy harm caused by criminal conduct through remedial steps to prevent or
address expected future harm and ensure the security of people in person and property, and
provide integrity to the system as a whole. Oregon’s sentencing guidelines define misdemeanor
offenses, presumptive felony convictions, and presumptive post-prison or probation supervision
violations. The commission reviews state legislation that creates new crimes or modifies
existing crimes, recommending guidelines for use in sentencing proceedings; and it makes
modifications to the crime seriousness scale of the sentencing guidelines to reflect legislative
action.

The chair of the Criminal Justice Commission is authorized to create committees within the
commission as thought necessary. The chair appoints committee members from all segments of
the criminal justice system that are affected by the committee’s work

Program: Administration

Program Description
The Administration program coordinates the work of the Criminal Justice Commission (CJC)
and its staff and programs. It supports the development and maintenance of CJC policies and
procedures, and ensures the appropriate use of public funds. The program provides operational
support of the organization’s administrative, financial, payroll, personnel, and commission
records.

Administration is led by the CJC’s executive director, who is appointed by the Governor.
Administration maintains the official copy of the records of the commission, and the Asset
Forfeiture Oversight Advisory Committee (see separate program descriptions).

Agency: Criminal Justice Commission
Program: Asset Forfeiture

Program Description
The Asset Forfeiture program lawfully takes property, contraband, and conveyances used to
commit or facilitate illegal conduct.

The Criminal Justice Commission (CJC) provides staff support to the Asset Forfeiture Oversight
Advisory Committee (AFOAC), a ten member group appointed by the Governor, Attorney
General, President of the Senate, and the Speaker of the House of Representatives. AFOAC is
responsible for collecting information on all state law forfeitures; receiving reports on the use of
forfeiture proceeds by public bodies; compiling detailed annual forfeiture reports; conducting
studies to improve the oversight of forfeiture; and making recommendations to increase the
effectiveness, fairness and efficiency of Oregon forfeiture actions. The CJC Administration
program (see above) maintains the official copy of AFOAC’s records.

Agency: Criminal Justice Commission
Program: Grants Management and State Administering Agency (SAA)

Program Description
The Criminal Justice Commission (CJC) allocates state and federal resources statewide for
comprehensive criminal justice planning and policy development.  This responsibility includes
public safety issue identification, system collaboration, policy development, and system planning
and implementation.  As the State Administering Agency (SAA) for the Edward Byrne Memorial
Justice Assistance Grant (JAG), the CJC maintains responsibility for distribution, monitoring, and
reporting of annual formula grants and when applicable, competitive grants.

The Edward Byrne Memorial Justice Assistance Grant (JAG) Program is the primary provider of
federal criminal justice funding to state and local jurisdictions. The JAG Program provides states
and units of local governments with critical funding necessary to support a range of program areas
including law enforcement; prosecution and court programs; prevention and education programs;
corrections and community corrections; drug treatment and enforcement; crime victim and
witness initiatives; and planning, evaluation, and technology improvement programs.

Further, the CJC serves as the primary coordinating body for the acceptance, planning, and
distribution of federal criminal justice funds leveraged to address the needs of the state criminal
justice system. Oregon’s SAA is an active member of the National Association of Drug Court
Professionals (NADCP) and the National Criminal Justice Association (NCJA).

Organizational Placement Schedule Number: 2016-0008
Agency: Criminal Justice Commission
Program: Oregon Knowledge Bank (OKB)

Program Description
The Oregon Knowledge Bank (OKB) is a resource for and created by the public safety
community in Oregon. The OKB focuses on Oregon: its innovative policing, corrections, and
community supervision programs; research conducted on criminal justice initiatives here; and
public safety agency profiles.  The Oregon Criminal Justice Commission (CJC) is responsible for
tasks related to sharing information and encouraging the use of best practices throughout the
state’s criminal justice system.  To do this, the CJC collaborated with stakeholders to establish
the OKB which launched in 2015.

The OKB is guided by two complementary goals: The first is to become the go-to resource for
criminal justice practitioners in Oregon for best and promising practices. The second goal is to
encourage public safety practitioners statewide to use evidence-based practices and problem-
solving methods.

Program: Local Public Safety Coordinating Council

Program Description
While LPSCCs across the state engage in a variety of activities to improve system-wide
communication and collaboration, their primary purpose is to: coordinate local criminal justice
policy and planning; make recommendations to the county board of commissioners regarding the
use of state and county resources to supervise local offenders; and develop and approve their
county’s Justice Reinvestment Grant program. County governments are required to send the
commission an annual summary of the changes made in response to the recommendations of its
LPSCC. In turn, the Criminal Justice Commission (CJC) is responsible to support and make
recommendations for effective utilization of LPSCCs within the coordinated state criminal justice
system.

Agency: Criminal Justice Commission
Program: Statistical Analysis Center (SAC)

Program Description
The Statistical Analysis Center (SAC) provides the Governor, Legislative Assembly, criminal
justice agencies, and the public with current and historical statistics, objective research, and data
analysis regarding Oregon’s criminal justice system. It directly supports the development of
legislative fiscal impact statements regarding proposed legislation that may increase or decrease
state costs for prison beds and offender supervision. SAC publishes criminal justice information,
statistics and research for use by state and federal criminal justice planners, state agencies,
universities, and the general public. It serves as a clearinghouse for the collection, preparation,
analysis and dissemination of information on statewide sentencing practices.

With oversight by the Criminal Justice Commission (CJC), SAC operates as the state’s criminal
justice collection, analysis and dissemination point under the nationwide SAC program operated
by the U.S. Department of Justice, Bureau of Justice Statistics. State SACs receive federal
funding to carry out data collection and analysis on topics of interest to criminal justice policy
and decision makers. Oregon’s SAC is an active member of the Justice Research and Statistics
Association (JRSA), a national nonprofit organization of state SAC directors, and other
researchers and practitioners, dedicated to policy-orientated research and analysis.

The CJC’s SAC uses and applies dynamic data and information from various government
agencies, including the Department of State Police, Oregon Department of Corrections, Oregon
Judicial Department, Oregon Youth Authority, and the Federal Bureau of Investigation.
